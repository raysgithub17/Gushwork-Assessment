Aluminum tubing offers an outstanding strength-to-weight ratio, making it significantly lighter than steel while still providing excellent structural integrity. It is highly resistant to corrosion, thermally and electrically conductive, and easy to machine or weld. These properties reduce overall product weight, lower transportation costs, and extend service life — making aluminum tubing a preferred choice across aerospace, automotive, and construction industries.

Aluminum tubing is primarily produced through an extrusion process, where a heated aluminum billet is forced through a shaped die to create the desired cross-section. The tube is then cooled, stretched for straightness, and cut to length. Additional processes such as heat treatment, anodizing, or drawing may be applied to achieve specific mechanical properties, surface finishes, or tighter dimensional tolerances.

Aluminum tubing is used across a wide range of industries. Common applications include aircraft frames and hydraulic lines in aerospace, chassis and heat exchangers in automotive, structural frameworks in construction, irrigation and fluid transport systems in agriculture, bicycle frames and sports equipment in consumer goods, and medical device housings. Its versatility and machinability make it suitable for both precision components and large structural assemblies.
